Mission context

Background, available data and programme of the December 2026 mission

Author
Affiliation

Renato Vargas

IMF Consultant

Published

November 24, 2026

Abstract

This note summarises the context of the IMF technical assistance to Tunisia on physical energy flow accounts (PEFA) and air emissions accounts (AEA). It describes the preparatory exchanges, the data provided by the authorities, the available tools and the programme of the mission planned for 7 to 11 December 2026.

1 Background

The technical assistance responds to a request from the Tunisian authorities for support on the compilation of air emissions accounts. It is part of the climate statistics programme of the IMF Statistics Department and involves the National Institute of Statistics (INS) and the relevant ministries.

In June 2026, Tunisian representatives took part in a three-day virtual workshop on energy and air emissions accounts. The workshop covered the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA) framework, energy balances and energy accounts, air emissions inventories and accounts, data requirements and compilation issues (Eurostat, 2015; United Nations et al., 2014; United Nations, 2019). The Tunisian participants presented their work and data (Besbes, 2026; Bouazizi, 2026).

2 The energy and emissions statistical system

The National Observatory of Energy and Mines (ONEM), under the ministry in charge of energy, produces the national energy balance. Tunisia has compiled a balance since the 1980s and has published it every year in the Eurostat and International Energy Agency format since 2010 (Bouazizi, 2026). The INS compiles and disseminates official statistics, including energy indicators and the national accounts.

The National Agency for Energy Conservation (ANME) coordinates the national greenhouse gas inventory for the energy and industrial processes sectors. The Ministry of Agriculture coordinates agriculture and land use, and the National Sanitation Office (ONAS) coordinates waste. The inventory covers 1990 to 2022, with an estimate for 2023, follows the 2006 IPCC Guidelines and uses the global warming potentials of the Fifth Assessment Report (Besbes, 2026; Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, 2006).

The INS reported three difficulties at the workshop (Bouazizi, 2026). The classifications of the energy balance and of economic activities are not always harmonised. Energy consumption by product and by sector has gaps and relies on estimates. The national accounts cover the informal sector, while the balance does not.

4 IMF tools

The IMF has developed two tools for these accounts. The PEFA Converter transforms an energy balance into physical energy supply and use tables using a concordance matrix between balance flows and industries (International Monetary Fund, 2024). The I2A converter transforms an emissions inventory into air emissions accounts by economic activity, with a residence adjustment for air and water transport. Both tools combine an Excel workbook with Python functions.

The processing pages of this site follow the same steps in documents that describe each operation and its code.
