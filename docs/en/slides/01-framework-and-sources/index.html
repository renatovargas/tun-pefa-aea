Energy and emissions accounts

The SEEA framework and the Tunisian sources

Renato Vargas
Achille Pegoue

What this presentation covers

  1. Why statistics on energy and emissions need accounts.
  2. The System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA) and its energy and emissions accounts.
  3. The three Tunisian sources that feed the accounts, and what each brings.

Why accounts?

Three systems describe the same economy

  • Energy statistics describe the supply and use of energy products on the national territory, by technical sector. In Tunisia, the national energy balance of ONEM.
  • Greenhouse gas inventories describe emissions on the territory, by process. In Tunisia, the inventory coordinated by ANME with the Ministry of Agriculture and ONAS.
  • National accounts describe the economy of resident units, by industry, in money. In Tunisia, the supply and use tables of the INS.

Each answers its own questions

System Typical question Unit of classification
Energy balance How much natural gas was burnt in power plants? Technical sector
Inventory How much methane came from livestock? Emitting process
National accounts How much value did the food industry add? Industry

None of them answers alone: how much energy does each dinar of value added require, and in which industry?

The questions policy asks

  • Which industries use the most energy, and the most energy per dinar of value added?
  • How much of household energy goes to transport, cooking or heating?
  • If the cement industry grows, how much do emissions rise?
  • Are energy use and emissions growing faster or slower than the economy?

These questions need energy and emissions organised like the national accounts.

The SEEA, an international standard

  • Adopted by the United Nations Statistical Commission in 2012 (United Nations et al., 2014).
  • A satellite of the System of National Accounts: same units, same classifications, same accounting rules.
  • Thematic guides for energy (United Nations, 2019) and air emissions; compilation manuals by Eurostat for both accounts (Eurostat, 2014; Eurostat, 2015).
  • Energy and air emissions accounts are among the SEEA accounts that countries compile most often.

Energy in the SEEA

Three kinds of energy flows

  • Energy from natural inputs flows from the environment into the economy: crude oil and gas when extracted, sun, wind, biomass.
  • Energy products circulate within the economy: produced, traded, transformed, used. Natural gas, diesel, electricity.
  • Energy residuals flow back to the environment: heat from burning fuels, electricity lost in the grid, gas flared at the well.

The flows in a picture

Energy is conserved

  • Energy is neither created nor destroyed; it changes form (United Nations, 2019, paras 2.14 to 2.21).
  • A power plant burns gas: part becomes electricity (a product), the rest is lost as heat (a residual).
  • A household uses that electricity: its energy ends as residual heat too.
  • So every use of energy has a matching residual, and the totals of the tables exceed the energy actually consumed. This is a feature, not an error.

Supply and use tables in physical units

Rows (flows) Supply: who provides it Use: who receives it
Natural inputs Environment Industries that extract
Energy products Industries, imports Industries, households, exports, stocks
Energy residuals Industries, households Environment, accumulation

For every row, supply equals use. Columns are the units of the national accounts.

Territory and residence

  • The energy balance records what happens on the territory.
  • The accounts record what resident units do, wherever they are (Eurostat, 2014, paras 41 to 48).
  • A Tunisian airline refuelling in Paris: in the accounts, not in the balance.
  • A foreign ship refuelling in Radès: in the balance (bunkers), not in the accounts.
  • A bridge table reconciles the two totals.

Emissions in the SEEA

  • Emissions are residual flows into the atmosphere (United Nations et al., 2014).
  • Recorded for the economic unit that emits: industries, and households as consumers (Eurostat, 2015).
  • Same residence principle as the energy accounts, with a bridge to the inventory total.
  • Land use, land-use change and forestry stay outside the accounts and appear in the bridge.

The Tunisian sources

Three sources

Source Producer Years Unit
National energy balance ONEM 2015 to 2022 ktoe
Supply and use tables INS 2015 to 2023 million TND
Greenhouse gas inventory (CRT) ANME and partners 2015 to 2022 TJ, kt

All three are used as received; the pipeline never edits them.

The energy balance

  • 45 flows and 23 products in 2022, in thousand tonnes of oil equivalent (1 ktoe = 41.868 TJ).
  • Supply (production, imports, exports, bunkers, stocks), transformation (power plants, refinery, charcoal kilns), own use, losses, final consumption by sector.
  • The layout changes between years: the pipeline reads labels, never cell positions, and stops on any unknown label.
  • Totals equal their parts to within 2.0 ktoe, the rounding of the published tables.

Production and imports

In 2022: 366 PJ of energy products from domestic production and 340 PJ from imports.

Reading the chart

  • Domestic production includes secondary products: electricity from power plants and products of the refinery.
  • Domestic production fell over the period while imports rose; imports exceeded domestic production in 2019.
  • Imports dipped in 2020, during the pandemic, and recovered in 2021.

The supply and use tables

  • 24 products and as many industries, in millions of dinars at current prices.
  • They give the accounts their columns: the industries of the national accounts.
  • Their purchases of fuels and electricity provide the keys that split road transport and the tertiary sector.
  • Their output and value added turn energy and emissions into intensities.
  • Supply equals use for every product to within 0.08 million TND.

The greenhouse gas inventory

  • One CRT workbook a year; the Data sheet holds every reported value in long format.
  • Emissions by category, fuel and gas, and fuel use in terajoules.
  • In 2022: 45.8 Mt CO2 eq excluding land use, and a land-use sink of -10.8 Mt.
  • Data matches the published Summary2 for every category, with one small exception (N2O of 1.B.2.b.ii from 2019).
