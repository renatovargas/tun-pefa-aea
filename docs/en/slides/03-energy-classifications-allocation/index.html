Energy accounts: classifications and allocation

From the rows of the balance to the industries of the national accounts

Renato Vargas
Achille Pegoue

What this presentation covers

  1. The rows and columns of the energy accounts: products, flows and activities.
  2. How each line of the balance finds its industry, and the two lines that need keys.
  3. The concordance matrix, and what the converter does with it.

Classifications

Rows and columns of the accounts

  • Rows: energy from natural inputs, energy products by group of the Standard International Energy Product Classification (SIEC), energy residuals (United Nations, 2018).
  • Columns: industries, households, accumulation, rest of the world, environment.
  • The IMF tool reports industries by ISIC section (A, B, C, D, E, F, H, other).
  • Tunisia uses the 24 industries of its supply and use tables, which add up to those sections.

Units

  • The balance is in thousand tonnes of oil equivalent (ktoe).
  • The accounts are in terajoules (TJ): 1 ktoe = 41.868 TJ (United Nations, 2018).
  • The IMF tool reports in petajoules (PJ): 1 PJ = 1,000 TJ.
  • The joule makes electricity, gas and oil additive by their energy content.

Flows: the balance in the IEA template

  • The converter reads the balance in the layout of the International Energy Agency: a fixed list of flows, in a fixed order.
  • Each Tunisian flow goes to one flow of the template; a few cells are exceptions.
  • Signs follow the IEA: exports, bunkers, transformation inputs and own use are negative.

Two lines need keys

  • Road transport is given by mode, not by user: trucks, buses, taxis and private cars belong to many industries and to households.
  • Commerce, administration, hotels is one line for several service industries.
  • Both are split with keys from the supply and use tables of the same year:
    • road: purchases of refined petroleum products (product 19);
    • tertiary: purchases of electricity and gas (product 35).

The keys in 2022

Reading the keys

  • Households receive 34% of road fuel, transport 27%.
  • Refining and electricity are left out of the road key, since their purchases of product 19 are mostly transformation inputs.
  • Keys change every year with the national accounts.
  • They assume a similar price per unit of energy for every user: the best available information, but an assumption.

The concordance matrix

What the matrix says

For each flow and product of the balance:

  • which PEFA product receives the energy,
  • in which table (supply or use),
  • for which activity,
  • with which share; the shares of a flow add up to one.

Default and Tunisian matrices

  • The default matrix of the tool allocates each flow to an ISIC section.
  • The Tunisian matrix replaces the rows of covered flows with one row per industry, with the factor of the key.
  • One matrix per year, because the keys change every year.
  • Imports, exports and stock changes keep the default rows.

What the converter does

  1. Gives each flow its heading (supply, transformation, own use, final consumption).
  2. Records primary production as a natural input from the environment, used by the extracting industry.
  3. Multiplies each value of the balance by its factors.
  4. Turns transformation outputs into supply of energy products.
  5. Creates, for every use of energy, a residual of the same size returned to the environment.

From origin to use in 2022

Reading the diagram

  • Natural gas goes mostly into power plants, which turn it into electricity.
  • Oil products go to households, transport, manufacturing and agriculture; crude oil is also exported.
  • Biofuels, mainly firewood and charcoal, are used by households.
  • Transformation appears as a use of products; its output reappears as domestic production.

Points to discuss

  • Is the level of 24 industries right, or would finer detail be useful?
  • Electricity autoproducers and recovered heat: chemical industry?
  • Solar heat: produced and used by households? Non-energy use (bitumen): construction?
  • Could ONEM split the own use of the energy industries by industry?
  • Are money purchases a good enough key for road transport and services?
